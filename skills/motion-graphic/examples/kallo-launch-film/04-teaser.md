# The teaser

Four days before launch, the team asked for a short pre-launch teaser: 5–10 s, one cut in English and one in Vietnamese, each with a demo meal in that language and the app set to that language. The owner wanted nothing on screen at the start that explains what the app is.

A teaser is a different genre from a launch film, so it got its own small study before any design.

## The study: 25 real teasers

Pre-launch teasers were pulled from Samsung, Notion, Nothing, Raycast, Google, Framer, CMF, Apple, Figma and teenage engineering. YouTube channels were listed with `yt-dlp --flat-playlist`, and the videos downloaded the same way. Cuts were counted by scene detection. Ten of the short ones were then broken down frame by frame with timestamped contact sheets and strips: what each one shows, what it hides, and when the words arrive.

Titles are shortened. Cuts are automatic scene-change counts.

## What the teasers have in common

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **6–14 seconds** | The short teasers (Samsung, Nothing, Notion, CMF) run 6–14 s. Raycast at 38 s and teenage engineering at 18 s play like trailers. |
| **0–1 cuts** | 8 of the 10 studied closely are a single continuous action: a hand cuts, a cursor clicks, dots morph. One move carries the whole clip. |
| **Text at 62–79% of the runtime** | That is where the only line of text appears in the Samsung "What's coming?" series, 3–4 words long. Before it, the object has to hook on its own. |
| **The name comes last** | The name or logo arrives in the last 1–4 seconds. None of them opens by saying what the product is. |
| **354K views on X** | Notion Mail, the most-viewed teaser in the set, shows a real UI glimpse for 2.6 s and then the "Mail" wordmark. The words live in the post text, not in the video. |

## The reveal ladder

Each level shows more of the product. The less people already know a brand, the higher it needs to go before a teaser means anything.

| Level | What is on screen | Examples | Fits when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Mystery** | Abstract shapes or a number; no product | Nothing "Updating…" | Followers already know you and are waiting |
| **2 · Metaphor** | An everyday object stands in for the product | Samsung "What's coming?" (Chocolate, Squeegee, Puzzle, Pizza: each an everyday object, no phone), teenage engineering "coming soon..." | The product's shape or idea is the news |
| **3 · Partial** | Tight crops of the real product, never the whole | Nothing "Control freak", Framer x Work Louder, CMF Buds | The product looks good up close |
| **4 · Glimpse and name** | One real action in the real UI, then the name and a date | Notion Mail, the Raycast teaser, Pixel 10a | A new app from a small account. People have to see what it does to care. |
| **5 · Demo** | A full walkthrough, features, people talking | Apple "dear diary", Figma "Slots" | Launch day, not before. That is the launch film's job. |

**Where Kallo sat: level 4, held to one meal.** The accounts were small (tens to a few hundred followers), so a mystery teaser would read as noise. But the launch film already did the full demo. The teaser shows one sentence turning into one number, then stops.

## Three directions

All three were 8 s, showed no text about the app until the end, were to be cut twice from real recordings (an English meal with the app in English, a Vietnamese meal with the app in Vietnamese), and ended on the wordmark and the launch date.

### A · The leak (the agent's pick, level 4)

A raw screen recording from the phone, as if someone posted it too early: status bar, no frame, no music bed, only the keyboard and a soft send sound.

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0.0–0.3 s | The recording starts mid-screen: an empty "Add a meal…" box, the keyboard up. |
| 0.3–3.6 s | The meal is typed letter by letter at real speed. This is the hook: people read along. |
| 3.8 s | Send. |
| 4.0–6.3 s | Two status words at most, then the rows land one by one with their kcal. |
| 6.3–6.8 s | The total lands. Hard cut on it. |
| 6.8–8.0 s | Black. The wordmark, small, and the date. |

Meals proposed: "a chicken caesar wrap and an iced oat latte" (English) and "1 dĩa cơm tấm sườn bì chả, thêm trứng ốp la và 1 ly trà đá" (Vietnamese: a plate of broken rice with grilled pork chop, shredded pork skin and egg meatloaf, plus a fried egg and a glass of iced tea).

- **Why:** it is a "leak", the same move as Nothing India's "(b)usted" self-leak. It looks nothing like the polished launch film, so it does not spoil it. And the sentence-to-number moment is the whole product in 4 seconds.
- **Risk:** a raw look can read as low effort, so the typing, the cut and the sound have to be precise.

### B · One sentence (level 4)

The Notion Mail grammar: only the input box on the soft gradient, no phone and no app chrome. The meal is typed (0.0–3.4 s), sent (3.6 s), the box opens into the result card and the rows land, the total holds (5.8 s), then the wordmark and the date (6.6–8.0 s).

- **Why:** the cleanest and most premium of the three.
- **Risk:** it is almost exactly the launch film's first 8 seconds, so launch day loses its surprise.

### C · The number (level 2–3)

The Nothing "Updating…" grammar in type only. The meal sentence is typed in large type in the centre of a plain frame (0.0–3.0 s). Its words fall away one by one, each leaving its kcal behind (3.0–5.6 s), until only the total remains (5.6–6.6 s). Then the wordmark and the date.

- **Why:** very readable as a sound-off feed post, and the numbers still come from real analyses.
- **Risk:** no real UI on screen, so it says less about the app, and it drifts toward a generic motion graphic.

Storyboard frames of all three, rendered from stand-in footage, are in [assets/storyboard/](assets/storyboard/) (`teaser-dir-a-leak.jpg`, `teaser-dir-b-sentence.jpg`, `teaser-dir-c-number.jpg`).

## The owner's flow

None of the three was used as proposed. The owner kept the level-4 idea and wrote the sequence themselves:

> In the canvas, video I think we can we can do it this way so first so show the home screen then click on to the uh, click on to the login page then start typing click send then after the new cards are out we zoom out click save then after the macros went up the total macro of the day went up in the guage uh, then we show the fade into the black screen with Kallo but don't show the the launch date please just say coming soon

("the login page" is the app's Log tab.) A day later:

> changes: make the VN version this meal: phở lườn gà không da, ít bánh, nước trong. and the end screen is coming soon. right now lets say no music, just sound effects.

Then the owner supplied a music track, which went back in (see below).

What this kept from the study: one continuous action (no cuts inside the demo), the real UI, the name last, no explanatory text. What it changed: it starts on the Home screen inside a room instead of a raw recording, it shows the save and the day's gauge going up (a second payoff after the meal's total), and it ends on "Coming soon" with no date.

## The final structure

Both cuts are 1080x1920 at 60 fps, laid out in the platforms' safe band (y 140–1620) with the noon room bleeding to the edges. Every frame of the phone is a recording of the real app on the development build. The meals and totals are the app's real results:

- **English:** "a chicken caesar wrap: about 120 g grilled chicken breast, a handful of romaine, 2 tbsp parmesan and 2 tbsp caesar dressing in a large flour tortilla, and a 16 oz iced latte with oat milk, no syrup". 813 kcal (P 51, C 67, F 38). The day goes from 1,932 kcal left to 1,119.
- **Vietnamese:** "phở lườn gà không da, ít bánh, nước trong". 393 kcal (P 42, C 40, F 6). The day goes from 1.932 to 1.539.

| Beat | English (12.4 s) | Vietnamese (10.6 s) |
|---|---|---|
| The whole phone on Home, the finger arrives | 0.0 s | 0.0 s |
| Tap the Log tab; the page pushes in | 0.65 s | 0.65 s |
| Tap into the composer; camera moves down to the input | 1.5 s | 1.5 s |
| The meal is typed, a letter at a time | 1.55–4.55 s | 1.55–3.55 s |
| Send; camera rises to the message and the status line | 5.0 s | 4.35 s |
| Two status words ("Connecting…", then one more) | 5.05–6.05 s | 4.4–5.0 s |
| The rows land; the card closes on its total | 6.05–7.9 s | 5.0–6.1 s |
| Zoom out to the whole phone | 7.75 s | 5.95 s |
| Save meal | 8.35 s | 6.55 s |
| "Meal saved"; camera to the gauge; the day's ring and macros fill | 8.6–9.55 s | 6.8–7.75 s |
| Fade to black (0.4 s) | 10.6 s | 8.8 s |
| Wordmark, then "Coming soon" / "Sắp ra mắt" | 11.1–11.75 s | 9.3–9.95 s |
| End | 12.4 s | 10.6 s |

Time was compressed honestly, the same way as in the film. Typing was recorded letter by letter and played faster (the English sentence is long). The wait for the analysis was cut down to two status words. The debug build's 0.7 s stall on Save was cut, and the jump it caused was bridged with a 0.3 s dissolve. Every number, row and animation that remains is what the app produced.

The Home screen is a full-resolution screenshot, not video: the simulator recorder drops frames when nothing on screen changes, so a still Home never reaches the file (see [06-production-notes.md](06-production-notes.md)). The push into the Log page is rebuilt from the real Home and Log frames.

## Aligning the music

The owner supplied Pixabay "Trailer Suspense" (#415585). Its structure: loud pulses until 76.5 s, a drop to near-silence, then a single hit at 79.4 s. That shape matches the teaser's: busy while the meal is typed and analysed, a held breath on Save, a hit on the name.

The track is offset so the hit lands 0.38 s after the fade to black starts, as the black completes and the wordmark begins to rise:

- English: track offset = 79.4 − (10.6 + 0.38) = 68.42 s. The drop then falls at 76.5 − 68.42 = 8.08 s of the teaser, 0.27 s before the Save tap at 8.35 s.
- Vietnamese: offset = 79.4 − (8.8 + 0.38) = 70.22 s. The drop falls at 6.28 s, 0.27 s before Save at 6.55 s.

So the Save tap and the gauge fill play in near-silence, and the music returns with the hit on the wordmark. The music sits at −6 dB under the effects, with a 0.25 s fade in and a 0.5 s fade out. An extra sub-drop effect on the wordmark was removed because the track's own hit did that job. Details in [05-sound.md](05-sound.md).
