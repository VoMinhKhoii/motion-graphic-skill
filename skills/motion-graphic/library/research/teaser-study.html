Kallo Teaser Study

Kallo teaser study · 7 Oct 2026 · 25 real teasers downloaded, 10 broken down frame by frame

How much should a 5–10 s teaser give away?

The team wants a short teaser before launch on Sunday 11/10. I pulled pre-launch teasers from Samsung, Notion, Nothing, Raycast, Google, Framer, CMF and teenage engineering and measured what each one shows, what it hides, and when the words arrive. Below: what they have in common, where each sits on a reveal ladder, and three directions for a Kallo teaser built around one real meal, cut once in English and once in Vietnamese.

What the teasers have in common

  • 6–14seconds

    The short ones (Samsung, Nothing, Notion, CMF) run 6–14 s. Raycast at 38 s and teenage engineering at 18 s play like trailers.

  • 0–1cuts

    8 of the 10 are a single continuous action: a hand cuts, a cursor clicks, dots morph. One move carries the whole clip.

  • 62–79%into the clip

    That's where the only line of text appears in the Samsung series, 3–4 words long. Before that, the object has to hook on its own.

  • last 1–4seconds

    The name or logo comes at the very end. Nobody in the set opens by saying what the product is.

  • 354Kviews on X

    Notion Mail, the most-viewed teaser in the set, shows a real UI glimpse for 2.6 s. The words live in the post text, not the video.

The reveal ladder

Each step up shows more of the product. The less people already know you, the higher up the ladder you need to go before a teaser means anything.

LevelWhat's on screenExamplesFits when
1 · MysteryAbstract shapes or a number, no productNothing "Updating…"Followers already know you and are waiting
2 · MetaphorAn everyday object stands in for the productSamsung "What's coming?", teenage engineeringThe product's shape or idea is the news
3 · PartialTight crops of the real product, never the wholeNothing "Control freak", Framer, CMFThe product looks good up close
4 · Glimpse + nameOne real action in the real UI, then the name and a dateNotion Mail, Raycast, Pixel 10aA new app from a small account. People have to see what it does to care.
5 · DemoFull walkthrough, features, people talkingApple "dear diary", Figma SlotsLaunch day, not before. That's the job of our launch film.

Where Kallo sits: level 4, held to one meal. Our accounts are small (X has 0 followers, Threads 73, TikTok 206), so mystery would read as noise. But the launch film already does the full demo. The teaser shows one sentence turning into one number, then stops.

Three directions for Kallo

All three are 8 s, show no text about the app until the end, and are cut twice from real recordings: an English meal with the app in English, and a Vietnamese meal with the app in Vietnamese. Each ends on the wordmark and the date.

A · The leak

My pickLevel 4

A raw screen recording from the phone, as if someone posted it too early: status bar, no frame, no music bed, only the keyboard and a soft send sound. A meal is typed in full, sent, and the real result lands. It cuts to black the moment the total appears.

  • 0.0–0.3 sThe recording starts mid-screen: an empty "Add a meal…" box, the keyboard up.
  • 0.3–3.6 sThe meal is typed letter by letter at real speed (the hook: people read along).
  • 3.8 sSend.
  • 4.0–6.3 sTwo status words at most, then the rows land one by one with their kcal.
  • 6.3–6.8 sThe total lands. Hard cut on it.
  • 6.8–8.0 sBlack. The Kallo wordmark, small, and "11.10".
English cuta chicken caesar wrap and an iced oat latte
Vietnamese cut1 dĩa cơm tấm sườn bì chả, thêm trứng ốp la và 1 ly trà đá

Why: it's the "leak" you asked about, the same move as Nothing's "(b)usted" self-leak. It looks nothing like the polished launch film, so it doesn't spoil it. And the sentence-to-number moment is the whole product in 4 seconds. Risk: a raw look can read as low-effort, so the typing, the cut and the sound have to be precise.

B · One sentence

Level 4

The Notion Mail grammar. Only the input box on the soft gradient, no phone and no app chrome. The meal is typed, sent, and the box opens into the result card. Then the wordmark and the date.

  • 0.0–3.4 sThe box on the gradient, the meal typed letter by letter.
  • 3.6 sSend. The box opens into the card; rows land.
  • 5.8 sThe total lands and holds for a beat.
  • 6.6–8.0 sWordmark and "11.10".

Why: it's the cleanest and most premium of the three. Risk: it's almost exactly the launch film's first 8 seconds, so launch day loses its surprise.

C · The number

Level 2–3

The Nothing "Updating…" grammar in type only. The meal sentence is typed in the centre of a plain frame, then its words fall away one by one, each leaving its kcal behind, until only the total remains. No UI at all.

  • 0.0–3.0 sThe sentence typed in large type.
  • 3.0–5.6 sEach food phrase collapses into its number; the numbers add up.
  • 5.6–6.6 sThe total alone.
  • 6.6–8.0 sWordmark and "11.10".

Why: it's very readable as a sound-off feed post, and the numbers still come from real analyses. Risk: no real UI on screen, so it says less about the app, and it drifts toward a motion graphic.

What happens after you pick

  1. Run both meals through the real app on the dev server (English app for the English meal, Vietnamese app for the Vietnamese one) and record the phone screen, typing a letter at a time.
  2. Storyboard the 8 s on the canvas for sign-off, with the exact totals the app returns.
  3. Render each language in 9:16 (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) and 4:5 (X, Threads), with sound.
  4. Post on L−2, Thursday 9/10, matching the playbook's teaser slot: the Vietnamese cut on Threads and TikTok, the English cut on X.
