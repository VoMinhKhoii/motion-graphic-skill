# Concepts: film ideas, not feature tours

A feature tour ("here is screen A, then screen B") is what amateurs make. A motion team pitches a film: a spine that carries the features, a motif only this product owns, a hook, a proof moment and an ending. This reference is how to generate those, and how to offer a range from on-brand to brave.

## The default is the owner's own design system

Start from what exists:
- the product's fonts, colours, radii, iconography, voice;
- its own gradients and illustrations;
- its own motion (how its sheets rise, how its numbers count).

The default concept looks like the product at its best: the brand, art-directed. The owner should never have to say "follow our design system". The worked example lost a round to a serif italic the brand did not use.

Then be brave in the options. Every concept set offers a range:

| Slot | What it is | Example (worked run) |
|---|---|---|
| **On-system** (1–2, one is the recommended default) | Pure brand: its type, colours and gradients; real UI; the motif drawn from the product | "One sentence": the meal types itself on the brand gradient and steps into the phone |
| **Stretch** (1–2) | Brand-compatible but adds a world: photography, time of day, a material, a character from the brand's own primitives | "One day": the same brand UI set in real rooms whose light moves from dawn to night. Chosen |
| **Wildcard** (0–1) | A bold idea that pushes the brand: a metaphor film, a cast of characters, a specimen collage | "The ring": food from every kitchen packs into the shape of the app's gauge, faster and faster |

Label each slot clearly, and recommend one with a reason.

## Ingredients of a concept

1. **A story spine.** The structure that makes the features feel like one film. Spines that work for software:
   - **A day / a week / a journey:** time passes and each step demos one feature; light, place or season changes ("One day").
   - **Before → after (the contrast):** the careless way vs this product's way, side by side, with the measured difference. The worked example's hook: a lazy one-line meal vs a detailed one, with the calorie gap as the payoff.
   - **One input, everywhere:** a single sentence or file follows through every surface (phone, web, share, team).
   - **Build the number:** real things assemble into the product's key metric or shape.
   - **The product as instrument:** a dark stage; the product's signals are the music.
   - **A rule acted out:** brand primitives become a cast that plays out the product's rule (playful style).
   - **The question and the answer:** a line poses the problem; each demo answers part of it.
   - **Someone tells it:** a founder or a customer speaks; the real UI is the b-roll under each claim (presenter branch, `branches.md`).
2. **An owned motif.** A visual element only this product has, carrying every transition: its ring gauge, its input caret, its brand dot, its card shape, its mascot. If you can swap in another company's logo and the film still works, there is no motif.
3. **A hook in 2 s.** Offer the owner one of two openings, with the tradeoff:
   - **Product gesture first:** frame 1 already shows the product doing something (typing, a toggle, a number flipping). It earns attention in a feed from strangers; most hook cards in the worked example's research (H1–H6) open on the product or its subject, not a name. The cost: the name arrives later.
   - **Brand intro first:** a short title ("Introducing X") on the brand ground, then the gesture within 2–3 s. It suits followers who already know the brand, a launch-day announcement, or an owner who wants the name heard first. The cost: 1–2 s that a stranger may scroll past.
   One card, H8, starts from the logo and rewrites it into the message: a middle path.
   The worked example opened on "Introducing Kallo" because the owner asked for it ("lets Introducing Kallo at middle, then transit to..."), then the product line, then a typed meal. The owner's approved storyboard beats any default here.
4. **A proof moment**, staged as the payoff. It can be a real number (a count-up, a gauge filling, two cards and the difference), a before and after, a workflow completed start to finish, or a customer outcome used with permission.
5. **Platform coverage.** How iOS, Android and web appear truthfully. For example, the phone's outline widens into a browser window and the same input carries over.
6. **Lines.** In a motion-design film, 4–8 short text cards, each 3–6 words; in a presenter or voiceover film the lines are the script (`branches.md`). A mix of:
   - the product line ("The text-first nutrition tracker, built for precision.");
   - feature lines ("We make it easy for you to relog a meal.");
   - a belief line ("At Kallo, we believe every journey works out better when we all feel supported.");
   - the end line ("Try it now / kallo.fit").
7. **The ending.** The wordmark plus one availability line in plain type, held in silence or on the music's hit.
8. **The research it rests on:** the technique cards (by code) each beat borrows.
9. **Cost:** imagery to make or shoot, takes to record, effort (low, medium, high).

## How to generate them

1. List the 3–6 moments the owner picked (Step 6), and the killer difference.
2. For each spine above, sketch in two sentences how it would carry those moments. Keep the 5–8 that are not forced.
3. For each survivor, find the motif in the product (the codebase dig lists the components and the product's own motion).
4. Check each against the research: which directions and cards support it, and what pace it implies (cuts a minute, text share).
5. Fill the slots: on-system, stretch, wildcard. Drop anything that is a feature tour in disguise.
6. Write each concept as a one-page board:
   - the title and a one-line logline;
   - the story in 3–5 sentences;
   - "what it proves";
   - platforms;
   - motif;
   - imagery;
   - effort;
   - 6–8 key frames: sketch frames from the engine or composites from real screenshots, never generic stock.
7. End with a comparison table and your recommendation.

## The worked example's five concepts

| Concept | Spine | Motif | Proof | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 · One sentence | One input, everywhere | The tan dot and the "shelf" line | The sentence breaks into ingredients; one detail changes the number | Medium |
| K2 · The ring | Build the number | The ring gauge made of food | The ring of food becomes the app's ring, filling with real numbers | High |
| K3 · One day (chosen) | A day | Light across the day (later, a hand-drawn clock) | Careless vs detailed meal, +245 kcal; then barcode, label, web, Circle, relog, share, micronutrients | Medium, needs new captures |
| K4 · The race | Before → after | The split race: a generic food database ("search, pick 1 serving, adjust grams") vs one sentence | The right-hand timer stops first and its answer is closer to what was eaten | Medium |
| K5 · Guess the plate | Question and answer | Empty slots, then the reveal: two plates, same words, one detail different | A beat of silence, then the real numbers land; round two left open for replies | Low |

The recommendation at the time was K1 for the launch, K2 as a no-UI teaser and K5 as a follow-up post. The owner chose K3, the calmest, because its time-of-day light read as more intuitive and premium once seen in motion. Recommend, but let the owner pick, and be ready for them to pick the stretch.

The five concepts are written up in `examples/kallo-launch-film/02-concepts/film-directions.md`.

## After the pick

Write the chosen concept as a beat list (time, what you see, the move, the line, the sound) and go to the storyboard. Expect the concept to evolve: in the worked example the owner rewrote the opening twice and added a teaser later, but the spine never changed.
