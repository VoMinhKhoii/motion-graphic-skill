# The review loop: versions, feedback, verification

A launch film is iterated, not delivered. The worked example went through nine film versions, a 9:16 cut and three teaser versions in eight days. These habits kept each round productive.

## Each round

1. **Parse the feedback** into numbered asks. Owners write fast; one message can hold fifteen asks across the film. Keep the timestamp or frame each refers to.
2. **Restate any ask you can't map to a frame**, and ask about that one only. Don't guess at "the frame at 17 s".
3. **Make the changes in the engine,** not in post.
4. **Storyboard the changed frames,** outline what changed, and send for sign-off.
5. **Render after sign-off.** Check the file: probe, volume, sampled frames.
6. **Reply with each ask closed:** what changed, where (time), and anything not done and why.

## How to read feedback

- "Janky": look for frame-level causes. Stalls, pops, mis-seeks and word-level typing all read as jank. Pull real frame timestamps and frames around the moment before changing timing.
- "Static" or "boring": add product motion (an incremental reveal, a small camera drift, the app's own animation), not more camera moves.
- "Too fast, moving around too much": count camera keys per second and hold still on the area being demoed.
- "Lost the elegance": compare the frames with the last approved version side by side; usually it is busier layouts, more text or lone objects.
- "Not that peak": go back to the references and measure what is different. Don't polish blindly.
- Praise is information too. Keep what was praised ("I like the confetti") and change only what was asked.

## Verify before claiming

- Look at frames from the final file, not the stills you rendered before the change.
- Run the always-checklist and this film's style checklist (`quality-bar.md`). Count what has a rule: for example status words per analysis (OCR or frame sheets), lines per text card, camera keys per scene.
- Probe audio levels and durations; the delivered duration matches the approved timeline.
- For external actions (uploads, publishing), confirm the result exists. If a step failed, say so plainly and offer the alternatives.

## Second opinions

An independent model or a teammate reviewing frames against the brief catches what you have stopped seeing. Give them the brief, the frames and the references. Make reviewers report-only, and verify their claims yourself.

## Keep a log

Write each round's feedback and changes to a log (`film/STATUS.md`, or a `film/log.md` it links to). It lets you explain a decision later ("why is the phone two-thirds?"), and it becomes the case study.
